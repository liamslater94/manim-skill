# Voice recording script: Mr. Liam IB Maths

About 4 to 5 minutes of reading. Each numbered **take** is about 10 to 15 seconds: the right size
for one voice sample. Read all of them in one recording, then upload it in Manim Studio under
⚙ Settings → Your voice → **Find my best clip**. The app tests every take and shows you the ones
that sound most like you.

## Before you press record

- **Same setup as your videos.** Same room, same microphone or phone, same distance (a hand's
  width, about 20 cm, slightly to the side of your mouth so you don't pop your p's).
- **Quiet and soft room.** Turn off fans and notifications. A room with curtains, a sofa or a
  wardrobe full of clothes sounds better than a kitchen or bathroom.
- **Teach, don't read.** Picture one student sitting next to you. Smile on the friendly lines,
  slow down on the important ones, and let questions go up at the end.
- **Leave two seconds of silence** before each take and after it.
- **Fluffed a word?** Pause, then say the whole sentence again. Don't stop the recording.
- Use your normal accent and your normal speed. The clone copies how you sound in the sample,
  so if you rush or go flat here, the videos will too.
- Save it as an **m4a** (Voice Memos is fine) or a **wav**.

## Take 1: Welcome
Hello, and welcome to Mr. Liam IB Maths! Today we're looking at a really nice question from
Paper One. It's one that catches a lot of students out, so let's work through it together.

## Take 2: Setting up
Here's the question. We have three equations and an unknown constant, eigh. Our job is to
find the values of eigh that give no solutions, and the values that give infinitely many.

## Take 3: Explaining a method
So, how do we start? The first thing I'd do is write the system as an augmented matrix. Then
we use row operations to reduce it to row echelon form, one step at a time.

## Take 4: Working through algebra
Take row two, and subtract three lots of row one. That gives us zero, minus four, minus seven,
and minus one. Now do the same with row three, but this time subtract five lots of row one.

## Take 5: Letters and symbols
We'll call the variables ex, why and zed. The direction vector is one, minus two, three, and the
parameter is lambda. Notice that zed appears in every equation, so we can eliminate it first.

## Take 6: A question to the viewer
Now, what do you think happens here? Pause the video and have a go yourself. Is the system
consistent? Does it have one solution, no solutions, or infinitely many?

## Take 7: Emphasis and warning
Be careful here. This is where most people lose marks. You must divide by eigh minus nine, but
only if eigh is not equal to nine. If you forget that condition, you'll miss a whole case.

## Take 8: Calculus words
Let's differentiate using the chain rule. The derivative of sine of three ex is three cos three
ex. Then we set it equal to zero, and solve for ex between zero and two pi.

## Take 9: Numbers
The answer is two point five, or five over two. That's fifteen percent, not fifty. The
probability is nought point nought seven, to three significant figures, from the year twenty
twenty-four.

## Take 10: More vocabulary
We'll use the scalar product, the cross product and the determinant. Then the modulus and the
argument of a complex number, de Moivre's theorem, logarithms, e to the ex, and sigma notation.

## Take 11: Squares, powers and roots
Ex squared, plus four ex, plus four, is ex plus two all squared. The square root of nine is
three. Two to the power of n, minus one, over n factorial. Plus or minus root three over two.

## Take 12: Encouragement
And that's it! If you got that right, really well done. That's a tricky question. If not,
don't worry at all. Go back to the step where it went wrong and try it again.

## Take 13: Summary
So, to summarise. When eigh is nine and the constant isn't zero, there are no solutions. When
both conditions hold, we get infinitely many solutions, and we can write them as a line.

## Take 14: Sign-off
Thanks for watching! If this helped, give it a like and subscribe for more IB Maths. Pick one of
these videos to keep learning, and I'll see you in the next one.
